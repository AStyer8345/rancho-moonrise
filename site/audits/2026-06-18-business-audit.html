2. Website

The Big One Landed — DNS Cutover:

The cap that held Website at B+ in April is gone. ranchomoonrise.com now serves the Vercel build directly — verified live this morning (HTTP 200, server: Vercel, apex canonical, www 308 → apex). Every blog post, schema tag, and landing page that was compounding authority on rancho-moonrise.vercel.app in April is now accruing to the real domain. This was the single highest-leverage open item across the last three audits and it shipped 04-30.

Schema Suite Is Effectively Complete:

The 17-post BlogPosting cluster now carries a full CreativeWork property set — wordCount, articleSection, ImageObject, inLanguage, isAccessibleForFree, copyrightYear, and copyrightHolder — layered on top of HowTo (corporate-retreat guide), ItemList (the numbered listicle posts), FAQPage, and BreadcrumbList. Internal linking sits at a 4-inbound floor with zero orphaned posts. A non-brand WebSearch for "glamping near Austin… safari tents pool" surfaced Rancho Moonrise in the AI answer — a citation it did not earn at baseline.

The Cap Now: Crawl, Not Build:

Two flagship landing pages are built and schema-complete but still uncrawled — /corporate-retreats/ (~45 days) and /safari-tents-near-austin/ (~50 days). The pages are done; Google just hasn't indexed them, and the gate is a 5-minute GSC URL-Inspection → Request Indexing that has been on Adam's plate for weeks. Until that happens, the schema work isn't fully cashing in.

Two Freshness Bugs Worth a Look:

The live homepage "Upcoming Events" cards show past dates (April 26, May 2, May 29) as "upcoming" — a visitor today reads stale events. The dynamic Supabase feed and the 06-09 popup are healthy, but the hardcoded homepage block needs a refresh or should pull from the live feed. Separately, the hero still reads "125+ Google reviews" while the live count is ~130 — a small but easy honesty bump. The 8 low-res source JPGs (four accommodation thumbnails at 340×340) still cap the responsive ladder and need re-upload from Ashley.

Next Actions:

1) GSC Request Indexing on the two uncrawled landing pages — 5 minutes, highest leverage. 2) Refresh or auto-wire the homepage "Upcoming Events" block so it never shows past dates. 3) Re-upload the 8 low-res images at 1600px+ and rerun the responsive pipeline.

7. HoneyBook

No Reported Change Since Baseline:

Wedding flow unchanged — 4 pages, 72+ vendors, full policy suite. Bar packages unchanged — three tiers ($7 / $11 / $15 per person per hour). No flows built for corporate retreats, private events, intimate events, or glamping packages. This area has not moved across either audit, and there's no evidence it moved during the pause.

Opportunity Cost Is Unchanged:

Private events are ~46% of revenue and POS/bar another ~22% — together roughly two-thirds of the business runs without a branded client flow. Every corporate inquiry still lands in a wedding-flow template or a raw email thread. A cloned wedding flow retargeted for corporate retreats would pay for itself on a single booking.

Next Actions:

1) Clone the wedding flow into a corporate-retreat flow — swap the policy language, drop in the corporate pricing structure, fork day-retreat vs overnight. 2) Add a side-by-side comparison table to the bar-package pages. This is the cleanest untapped revenue lever in the whole audit.

The inquiry CRM webhook wiring (see Booking) is as much an ops win as a booking win — it's the first piece of real lead-capture plumbing the business has had. The 06-09 events-popup feature added admin-side control (a show_in_popup column and Feature toggle), so Ashley can light up a homepage modal without code. The scheduled-task backbone keeps running — SEO daily, competitive weekly, review monitor, GBP posts — and the live-verify gates in those runs are catching real deploy state.

The Pause Is Now an Ops-Hygiene Problem:

Rancho has been "paused / cruise-control only" since the May 18 GOALS freeze — ~30 days. In that window rancho-site-daily alone has fired 19 consecutive times with no shipped edits, and concurrent writers have left orphaned run-logs and duplicate artifacts in the tree that every run now has to carefully avoid staging. The 30-second fix — add these tasks to the GOALS pause list so they exit cleanly — has itself been open ~22 days. The tasks aren't doing harm, but they're generating noise and a small amount of avoidable mess.

No GA4/GTM measurement ID, so conversion events fire to console.debug and report nowhere — the business still can't see which channel drives bookings. GITHUB_TOKEN on Vercel is still broad-scoped (a 5-minute fine-grained-PAT rotation). And the Paul/Donna step-away / governance conflict from the April 23 onsite is still unresolved — Donna acting unilaterally, the bookkeeper unusable as a financials channel, Exhibit A still missing. That governance overhang is the single biggest non-technical risk on the board.

Next Actions:

1) Pick a side on the paused tasks — add them to the GOALS pause list or remove them from the registry (30 seconds, ends the daily no-op fires). 2) Supply a GA4 or GTM ID so the conversion plumbing actually reports. 3) Rotate the GITHUB_TOKEN. 4) Get Nancy's read on the governance conflict so the buyout model can move.

9. Bottom Line

This audit covers 56 days, not the usual 14 — the bi-weekly cadence broke when Rancho went to paused / cruise-control on May 18, and this is the first audit since April 23. Over that long window, the picture splits cleanly: the things that were code, shipped; the things that were a manual push or an external claim, didn't.

The two bets the April audit named both landed. DNS cutover happened 04-30, so the entire schema-rich Vercel build now serves from ranchomoonrise.com — that alone moves Website B+ → A-. And the inquiry-form backend got wired, so submissions hit a CRM webhook instead of vanishing — Booking C+ → B. Layered on top is a near-complete BlogPosting schema suite and the first non-brand AI citation Rancho has earned. Real progress, and it's on the board.

The misses are equally clear, and two are essentially free. TripAdvisor is still 0 reviews and unclaimed (confirmed live today), Hipcamp is still 0, WeddingWire still has no listing — Reviews stays F, untouched for 70 days. Worse, April's clean "unreplied = 0" GBP win regressed: a 5★ and a 110-day-old Knot 1★ both sit with replies written and unposted, which pulls GBP back a half-step to B-. None of these are hard. Claiming TripAdvisor is 20 minutes. Posting two drafted replies is five. The bottleneck, again, is the push — not the plan.

The bet for the next audit: post the two waiting replies, claim TripAdvisor, open WeddingWire, request indexing on the two uncrawled landing pages, and make the ResortPass call. Every one of those is under 30 minutes and none requires a single line of code. If they happen, the next audit reads Reviews F → C, GBP B- → B+, Website A- → A. If they don't, we'll have learned that the constraint on this business was never the build — it was thirty minutes of someone's afternoon.
