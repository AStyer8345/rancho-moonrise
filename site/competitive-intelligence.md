**Rancho Moonrise is ranking on non-brand commercial keywords for the first time in this report's history.**

The "publish your own guide/listicle content" lever this report has recommended since May — modeled on Spoon Mountain and Cameron Ranch — is the thing that actually broke the drought. **Lucky Arrow raised day-pass pricing 17%** — Adult Day Pass and Family Pool Day Pass both moved $30 → **$35**. The other four products and all operating hours are unchanged; the listing shows 4.6★ across 200 reviews.

Together those two produce the sharpest pricing read this report has surfaced: **Rancho sells a $20 day pass at 4.8★ (53 reviews) against Lucky Arrow's $35 at 4.6★ (200 reviews)** — 43% cheaper with a higher rating, against a competitor that has just demonstrated the market absorbs an increase.

**The corporate cluster is getting crowded at exactly the moment Rancho entered it.** Three new entrants: **Serana** (Paige TX, 8 units, $1,000/night full-property weekday) at ~#3 — the closest analogue to Rancho's buyout model, and it publishes a flat price where Rancho custom-quotes; **7744 Ranch**, which markets "just east of Austin — approximately 20 minutes from downtown," **Rancho's exact geographic hook**, though capped at 100 guests / 10 overnight against Rancho's 200 / full-ranch lodging; and **Artemis Ranch**.

Structural gaps: Hipcamp curated set **re-stabilized** (top 8 byte-identical to July 15, Lucky Arrow holds #8), Rancho absent for the **8th consecutive read**. Glamping Hub absence now ~**18 weeks**. Two new earned-media surfaces joined Tribeza on brand queries: a **Yahoo News video feature** and **MTHR Collective**.

## Section 2 — Competitor Highlights

### The Week's Story — Rancho Entered the SERP, and the Blog Did It

For four months this report has recommended own-domain guide content on the strength of competitor evidence: Spoon Mountain ranking #1 on romantic terms with its own guide, Cameron Ranch ranking #2 on weekend-getaway with its own roundup. The recommendation was always inferential — no Rancho page had ever ranked, so the thesis was untested on this property.

It is tested now. `/blog/corporate-retreat-near-austin/` ranks ~#4 on the head corporate term, **above Rancho's own purpose-built `/corporate-retreats/` landing page** at ~#6. `/blog/weekend-getaways-near-austin/` ranks ~#4–5 across two weekend-getaway queries with no landing-page support at all.

Two things follow. First, the 18-post blog cluster is a live commercial asset, not a content-marketing nicety — and it produced these results **while paused since 2026-04-23**. Second, the months of schema/AEO enrichment work (`wordCount`, `articleSection`, `ImageObject`, `speakable`, FAQPage) landed on exactly the pages that are now ranking. The two workstreams converged.

### Pricing — Rancho Is Materially Underpriced on Day Access

Direct comparison of the two ResortPass listings this report tracks:

|---|---|---|
| Adult day pass | **$20** | **$35** (was $30 — raised this cycle) |
| Half-day pass | $15 | — |
| Cabana | — | $175 (4 guests) |

| Rating | **4.8★** | 4.6★ |

Rancho is **43% below** the nearest comparable day-pass product while carrying a higher guest rating. Lucky Arrow just raised its price and its listing continues to accumulate reviews, which is direct evidence the market tolerates the higher number. Rancho's 53 reviews (up 8 since July 15) show the channel is converting.

This is a revenue lever that requires no build, no content, and no indexing — only a decision.
