## 1. ResortPass — +8 reviews on a surface nobody monitors

**This is the finding of the run.**

ResortPass is not in `master-agent.md`'s live-claim ownership table and has never
appeared in `brand/review-aggregate.json`. It does not appear anywhere under `brand/`
or `tasks/review-monitor/`. It has been accumulating reviews unmonitored.

It now carries **53 reviews at 4.8★** — the second-largest review pool Rancho has
after Google, larger than The Knot (8) and Facebook (6) *combined*, by a factor of four.

The structural point: **`resortpass.com` returns HTTP 200 to a plain fetch and parses
cleanly.** Six of eight in-scope platforms are hard-blocked (Google, Airbnb, Hotels.com,
The Knot, Hipcamp, TripAdvisor). The one review surface that is actually scrapeable is
the one that was never being scraped.

The rating moved **4.9 → 4.8** across those 8 reviews. That is not alarming on its own —
8 new reviews averaging ~4.2 would do it — but it is the only rating decline observed
on any platform this year, and it is invisible to every current monitor.

**Individual review text is not exposed.** The listing page renders the aggregate only;
`/hotels/rancho-moonrise/reviews` is a 404. No per-review enumeration path was found,
so no reply-coverage state can be established and **no response drafts are possible**.

→ **FLAG_FOR_ADAM.** Two questions, both ~2 minutes in the ResortPass host dashboard:
1. Are there unreplied reviews sitting in there? 53 reviews with zero reply coverage
   is a real gap, and nobody has looked.
2. Do the 8 new reviews explain the 4.9 → 4.8 drift, or is there a specific bad one?

→ **Recommend adding ResortPass to monitored scope** (see run-log; requires a
`master-agent.md` ownership-table edit, which is a scope change, not a data write —
not made unilaterally this run).
