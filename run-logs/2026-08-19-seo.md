## 1. Why `sameAs`, and why it is not a ranking play

Yesterday's baseline (`serp-baseline-2026-08-18.md`) measured the on-page levers exhausted: inbound-link count correlates *inversely* with ranking, and word count + schema depth are uniform across ranking and non-ranking pages alike. Today added a third disconfirmed on-page lever (§3). What is left on this property is not ranking-shaped — it is **entity-shaped**, and the 8/18 baseline named the exact failure:

> The synthesized answer for the bare brand query described the property as *"20 luxury cabins and safari tents for up to 200 guests"* — copy `VOICE-GUIDE.md` bans twice over ("luxury", specific unit count).

**Diagnosis.** The site's own entity description is clean and correct:

> `"Austin's glamping and events ranch offering hand-crafted cabins, safari tents, weddings up to 200 guests, corporate retreats, live music events, and a resort-style pool on 36 acres, 20 minutes from downtown Austin."`

The site is not failing to describe itself. It is failing to **claim its own listings**. Sitewide there was exactly **one** `sameAs` array — `index.html:93` — carrying four social profiles and **zero** review, booking, or OTA profiles:

```
instagram · facebook · tiktok · linkedin
```

So every surface that actually carries a review pool or a bookable rate — ResortPass (53 reviews, Rancho's 2nd-largest pool), Hipcamp, The Knot, TripAdvisor, Yelp, Hotels.com — sat **outside the declared entity graph**. An engine reconciling "what is Rancho Moonrise" found the owned domain as one node among many peers, rather than as the hub those listings hang off. `sameAs` is the schema.org property that says *those listings are me*.

**Stated plainly so it is not over-sold:** `sameAs` is **not a ranking factor** and this edit will not move a single position. It is a knowledge-graph / AEO signal. Given that AI-engine citation coverage (4/10) is now a tracked metric and on-page ranking levers are measured dry, entity consolidation is the correct remaining category — but it is a slow, indirect lever and should be reported as one.

## 3. A third on-page lever tested and disconfirmed: exact-match title/H1

The obvious untested hypothesis after 8/18 was that ranking pages carry closer keyword matches in `<title>`/`<h1>`. Measured across the cluster — **it fails, and it fails backwards:**

| Target keyword | Page | Title match | Ranks? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pool day pass Austin TX` | `pool-day-pass-austin` | **near-exact** — "Austin Pool Day Pass — Resort Pool Access Near Austin, TX" | **no** |
| `things to do Manor TX` | `things-to-do-manor-tx` | **exact** | **no** |
| `glamping near Austin TX` | `glamping-near-austin-texas` | **exact** | **no** |
| `unique wedding venues near Austin` | `wedding-venues-near-austin` | **exact** — "Unique Wedding Venues Near Austin TX" | **no** |
| `overnight event venue Austin` | `wedding-venues-near-austin` | **absent from title** (H1 only: "…With Overnight Stays") | **yes — 4/8, best in set** |

The sharpest case is one page appearing twice: `wedding-venues-near-austin.html` **misses** the keyword sitting in its own title, and **ranks best in the entire set** on a keyword that is not in its title at all.

**What the data actually points at.** Sorting the 10 keywords by who holds the top of each set, the split is clean:

- **Rancho ranks** where the SERP is *fragmented* — `bachelorette party Austin ranch` is held by `ask.metafilter.com`, `premierpartycruises.com`, `fearlesscaptivations.com`.
- **Rancho is absent** where the SERP is *aggregator-consolidated* — Glamping Hub, Hipcamp, Booking, Cvent, EventUp, Eventbrite, The Knot, ResortPass.

Ranking correlates with **competitive weakness of the SERP**, not with any on-page attribute measured so far (links, words, schema depth, title match). Three levers now tested, three disconfirmed.

**The strategic consequence, which is uncomfortable:** on aggregator-owned head terms, on-page work cannot win, because the winners are not pages — they are *platforms*. The lever for those terms is **being in the aggregator**, not out-ranking it. Which means the two items that have sat lowest-priority in `TODO.md` for months — **Glamping Hub submission (~18 weeks, free, 15 min)** and the **Hipcamp curation gap** — are, on this evidence, worth more than any remaining on-site edit. That is a reprioritization, not a new task.
